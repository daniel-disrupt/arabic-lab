## Flashcards tab (new)

Each card shows a proverb in Arabic — tap the info button to flip the card and see the literal translation
and meaning, and star anything you want to revisit. Finish a round and only
the starred cards come back for the next one, so you can keep drilling until
nothing's left starred.

---

## Fill in the Blank tab (new)

Each proverb is missing one word — pick the right one from the choices to
complete it, then tap More to check the meaning.

---

## Word Scramble tab (new)

The proverb's pieces are scrambled — tap them in order to rebuild it, then
check the meaning once you've solved it.

## About tab

### Section 1 (existing — edit if you want, otherwise leave as is)

**Eyebrow:** Why proverbs
**Heading:** A lot said in a few words

Proverbs are beautiful because they compress so much into so little — a
handful of words carrying real weight, and along the way teaching you what a
culture values and how it sees the world.

They're worth learning because pulling out the right one, for the right
person, at the right moment, is its own small act of connection. It surprises
people, it makes them smile, and it shows you've actually gone deep into
Palestinian culture — not just the vocabulary, but the sensibility behind it.
And the words themselves stay useful well beyond the proverb they came from.

### Section 2 (existing — edit if you want, otherwise leave as is)

**Eyebrow:** Why these proverbs
**Heading:** Proverbs I'd actually say

I picked proverbs that map onto day-to-day life — neighbors, patience, hard
work, luck — rather than ones I'd never realistically reach for.

I've also mostly left out proverbs that lean on overt religious
framing. I'm not Muslim, I'm not religious, and I wouldn't talk that way in
Hebrew. So I gravitate towards proverbs that are more universal and secular.

### Section 3 (new/proposed)

Modeled directly on the one already in the Abed lesson's About tab (eyebrow
"How I Built This" / heading "The process, and where AI came in"), adapted
for this lesson:

**Eyebrow:** How I Built This
**Heading:** The process, and where AI came in

AI did a lot of the heavy lifting here: researching and sourcing proverbs
that fit that bar, translating each one into Hebrew and English, drafting
the tashkeel that shows how it's actually pronounced, and generating the
audio you can tap to hear. I also used an AI coding assistant (Claude Code)
to build the site itself — the flashcards, fill-in-the-blank, and
word-scramble tools all came together through that back-and-forth. I had
several trusted human experts check my work along the way — confirming I'd
picked the right proverbs, contributing a few of their own favorites, and
helping fine-tune them for how the dialect is actually spoken.

For a detailed technical write-up of that process — including what went
wrong along the way and what I learned about working with agentic AI — see
AI-PROCESS.md (same doc/link the Abed lesson already uses).
